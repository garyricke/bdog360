01 · What to make

Five content pillars

Every video should be assignable to one of five buckets. This keeps the feed varied, makes batch-filming easy, and ensures the channel always balances emotion (stops the scroll), education (earns the follow), and action (drives the click). The three marked “produced” are the samples already shipped.

Pillar 01

Relief Stories

Short, human “before → after” moments — the weight lifting when a payment gets cut. The emotional hook that makes a stressed viewer stop and feel seen.

Goal: stop the scroll, build trust · Format: UGC / testimonial

Pillar 02

60-Second Save

A quick screen-led walk through the savings estimate: “Here’s what $30k could look like.” The most direct path from curiosity to the website calculator.

Goal: drive clicks to the site · Format: demo / hook

Pillar 03

Myth-Busting & Tips

“What debt collectors won’t tell you,” “Settlement vs. consolidation in 30 seconds.” Saveable, shareable education that earns authority and follows.

Goal: reach + follows · Format: talking-head / list

Pillar 04

Local · Fox Valley

“Helping Batavia & Fox Valley families get out from under debt.” Geo-anchored trust that paid targeting can amplify to a 25-mile radius.

Goal: local trust + targeting · Format: b-roll / voiceover

Pillar 05

Meet the Negotiator

Real faces from the team answering one real question per clip. Humanizes the brand and pre-sells the “a real person is on your side” promise.

Goal: humanize + convert · Format: face-to-camera Q&A

02 · How many

Twelve to sixteen a month — filmed in two days

Consistency beats polish on these platforms. The target is 3–4 posts per week, produced in two monthly batch-film days so the team isn’t scrambling for daily content. One filmed asset is cut and reframed for TikTok, Reels, and Shorts — so ~12–16 produced videos become ~36–48 posts across channels.

The 80/20 split.

Roughly 80% of clips should be emotion + education (Pillars 1, 3, 4, 5) to earn reach and trust; ~20% direct demo/CTA (Pillar 2). A feed that only sells gets throttled by the algorithm and ignored by viewers — the value content is what buys the right to ask for the click.

03 · How it drives to the website

Every clip is the top of the funnel

A video that gets views but no clicks is a vanity asset. Each post is built around one job: move a stranger from “that’s me” to a completed savings estimate on the site. The structure that does it:

Hook (0–3s)

Name the pain in the first sentence — “If your minimum payments keep going up, watch this.” Win the scroll before anything else.

Value (3–40s)

Deliver the story, tip, or quick demo. Show the relief or the number. Make them feel understood and a little hopeful.

Call to action

One clear ask, every time: “Check your free estimate — link in bio.” Reinforce with an on-screen caption and a pinned comment.

Land on the calculator

The link goes straight to the website’s savings calculator (the offer), not the homepage. Tracked with UTMs so we know which clip earned the lead.

Closing the loop.

Link-in-bio → /#calculator with per-pillar UTM tags · pin the link in comments · turn the website carousel into retargeting fuel · run paid spend only behind clips that already proved a strong organic hook rate. The website and the channel feed each other: clips drive new visitors in, and the on-site carousel keeps them watching once they arrive.

04 · The scripts

What actually stops the scroll

A polished face talking calmly to camera is exactly what people swipe past — that’s the trap the first samples fell into. Short-form rewards tension, specificity, and a reason to stay. The job of frame one isn’t to look nice; it’s to make scrolling feel like a mistake. Below: the rules we write to, then six scripts built on them — each a different native format, none of them an ad that looks like an ad.

0–1sOpen mid-action on a hook — a number, a claim, or a screenshot. No logo, no “hey guys.”
Open a loopPose a question the viewer needs answered. They stay to close it.
Be specific“$8,000 at 24%” beats “a lot of debt.” Real numbers feel true.
Pick a villainThe minimum payment, the collector, the consolidation loan — not the viewer.
One askEvery clip ends on the same destination: the free estimate. Captions always on.
