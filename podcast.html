Channel Plan · Audio

A podcast that turns "I'm scared to look" into "let me run my numbers."

Debt is a topic people research in private, late at night, in their own headphones — which is exactly where a podcast lives. Debt, Decoded is a short, plain-English show that builds trust at scale, feeds the short-form video machine with clips, and sends warm listeners to the savings calculator. Below: the format, five episodes to launch with, how it distributes, and a fully produced sample.

01 · The format

Two voices, one topic, twelve minutes

Each episode is a tight, conversational "audio overview" — two friendly co-hosts unpacking a single debt topic from a Bulldog 360 source brief. No celebrity guests to book, no studio day required: we write the brief, the hosts handle the back-and-forth, and we publish. It's the fastest credible way to stand up a real show.

Length

8–14 minutes. Long enough to teach one thing well, short enough for a commute or a dog walk.

Tone

Two co-hosts, warm and plain-spoken. Curious-friend energy, never a lecture. One explains, one asks the questions a listener would.

Source-driven

Every episode is built from a vetted Bulldog 360 brief, so the facts, rights, and numbers stay accurate and on-message.

Repurposable

One recording becomes the episode, 3–5 short video clips, and quote cards — feeding the TikTok / Reels / Shorts plan.

03 · Cadence & distribution

Publish every two weeks, everywhere at once

A biweekly cadence is sustainable and keeps a back catalog building. One recording is distributed as audio, embedded on the site, and sliced into short video — so the podcast and the video channel feed each other.

Transcript

Host A

Welcome back to Debt, Decoded. So picture it — it’s 2 a.m., you can’t sleep, and you’re doing that thing where you add up all your credit card balances in your head.

Host B

[laughs] Oh, the worst math in the world. And here’s the part that keeps people up: the minimum payment. It feels responsible, right? You’re paying on time.

Host A

Totally. But the minimum is kind of… a trap?

Host B

It really is. Let’s make it concrete. Say you’ve got $8,000 on a card at 24% APR.

Host A

Which is a super common rate right now.

Host B

Right. You pay only the minimum, every single month. Want to guess how long until it’s paid off?

Host A

I mean… a few years? Maybe five?

Host B

Twenty-seven years.

Host A

Twenty-seven?! [sighs] I’m sorry, what?

Host B

Twenty-seven years. And you’d pay over $11,000 in interest, on top of the original eight.

Host A

That is wild. So the minimum payment is basically designed to keep you paying forever.

Host B

That’s the business model. The minimum keeps the account current, but it barely touches the principal. Most of it is just interest.

Host A

Okay, so what actually breaks the cycle?

Host B

A few options, and they are not all equal. Consolidation, settlement, bankruptcy. We’ll do the honest comparison next episode. But the short version: you want a plan that attacks the principal, not one that just moves the debt around.

Host A

And there’s a faster lane than 27 years.

Host B

Way faster. Programs that negotiate the balance down can wrap up in 24 to 48 months instead.

Host A

So if you’re lying awake doing that 2 a.m. math, the move isn’t to white-knuckle the minimum.

Host B

The move is to run your actual numbers. There’s a free 60-second estimate — no credit hit — that shows what you could save. Start there.

Host A

Debt, decoded. We’ll see you next time.
