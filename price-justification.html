01 · Quick Primer · How Debt Settlement Makes Money

Three facts you need before any of the pricing makes sense.

1. The customer is a household with too much credit-card debt. The typical enrolled client has $10K–$50K of unsecured debt and is struggling to pay it down. They sign up because they want one monthly payment and a path out — not bankruptcy, not a 5-year minimum-payment grind.

2. The business model is performance fees, not subscriptions. Debt-settlement firms charge a fee equal to roughly 15–25% of the enrolled debt amount. On a $25K enrollment that's $3,750–$6,250 of revenue per customer, paid as each individual creditor is settled (usually over 24–48 months). The fees are regulated — the FTC requires that fees only get charged after a settlement is actually reached.

3. Every enrollment is high-LTV, but expensive to acquire. Because each enrolled customer is worth thousands of dollars in fees over the life of their program, the entire industry tolerates customer acquisition costs (CAC) that would look insane in most other businesses. National Debt Relief pays $335+ per acquired enrollment. That's the baseline. Bulldog's whole strategic premise is undercutting that.

✦

The mental model

Don't think of the $319K as "the price of a website plus some ads." Think of it as the all-in cost of standing up a customer-acquisition factory in a category where each acquired customer is worth roughly $5,000 in lifetime revenue. The question isn't "is $319K a lot of money?" — it's "how many enrollments does $319K need to produce to pay for itself?" The answer, even pessimistically, is fewer than three months of pipeline.

Payback in a single number.

Run the unit economics on the plan as a whole before defending any individual cost. Every line below is grounded in numbers already published in the growth plan and in industry benchmarks (NDR public CAC, FTC fee-rule guidance, the $6.1B → $11.2B market projection).

The pricing isn't a bet that the customer succeeds wildly — it's the floor cost of having a real shot at all. Everything above 64 is profit on the marketing investment.

03 · Stage 1 · The $55K Build

What you're actually buying when you spend $55K once.

This is the only one-time cost in the whole 12-month plan. It builds the asset that every later dollar of marketing flows through. Without it, none of Stage 2 or Stage 3 produces enrollments — the current bdog360.com is a brochure with no calculator, no CRM, no escrow, no retargeting pixels. Spending paid-media dollars on a static brochure is lighting money on fire.

Where the $55K goes

$20K–$30K

Multi-step calculator + portal build

UI/UX, front-end, back-end. This is the actual conversion engine — qualifies debt > $10K, creditor count, employment, bankruptcy status. Same pre-qual structure that Freedom and NDR use to keep their CAC down.

$5K–$15K

Forth CRM integration

Forth is the industry-standard debt-settlement CRM. Includes Forth Credit (soft-pull credit verification, no FICO hit) and Forth Pay (FTC-compliant escrow). Without this the sales team works in spreadsheets — non-starter at any scale.

$3K–$8K

Payment / escrow gateway

ACH integration for the regulated escrow account every enrolled customer needs. Not optional — this is how the FTC says the money has to move.

$5K–$15K

Security & compliance baseline

Encryption, GDPR/CCPA configuration, basic pen test. You're handling SSNs and debt data; one breach ends the business.

Why $55K is the right tier — not the cheap tier and not enterprise

The market range for portals like this is wide: $40K on the low end, $150K+ on the enterprise end. Picking the right tier matters more than picking the cheapest.

Going below $40K — say, a $15K WordPress freelancer build — saves cash today and torches the next 12 months. No Forth integration, no escrow, no PURLs, no soft-credit pull. Every Stage-2 dollar produces less than half the enrollments it should because the funnel leaks.

Going above $80K means paying for enterprise features Bulldog 360 doesn't need yet — multi-tenant white-label dashboards, AI negotiation engines, multi-gateway billing. Those become real in Year 2 once Stage 3 productization kicks in. Buying them on Day 1 is paying for the future before the present works.

$55K lands on the floor of "mid-range done right": calculator, Forth, PURLs, escrow, security. Nothing extra, nothing missing.

✦

Stage 1 payback

$55K ÷ $5K per enrollment = 11 enrollments to repay the build entirely. At the plan's target rep pace of 3–5 enrollments per week, the build is paid back inside the first month of Stage 2 — before any of the monthly marketing has even fully cycled.

Continuous vulnerability scanning plus monthly legal review of mail copy and ad creative under FCRA + FTC rules. The reason this isn't optional: a single non-compliant prescreened mailer can get Bulldog cut off by the credit bureaus for years.

✦

The $22.5K/month gut-check

$22.5K/month is less than the fully-loaded monthly cost of a single mid-level marketing hire in a Chicago suburb (salary + taxes + benefits + tools). For the same money the customer gets a full media program, a CRM stack, a content engine, an active lead supply, and compliance coverage. The choice isn't "spend $22.5K/mo or save $22.5K/mo" — it's "rent a working acquisition system for $22.5K/mo, or hire one marketing manager and watch them try to build it themselves." The first option produces enrollments. The second produces meetings.

05 · Stage 3 · $43K/Month — Only If Earned

The $43K never gets spent unless the customer is already winning.

Stage 3 is conditional, not committed. The plan locks a hard CPA gate: blended customer acquisition cost must come in under $100 for two consecutive months before any spend escalates above $22.5K. If the gate isn't met, Stage 3 simply doesn't happen — Bulldog stays at $22.5K/month and the team diagnoses the funnel instead of pouring fuel on it.

Why each line grows the way it does

The Stage 3 increases aren't proportional — each line scales by what it needs to scale by once volume goes up.

  • Paid media (+$5K): The funnel works; pour more fuel. Programmatic display and YouTube retargeting both have headroom before CPA inflates.
  • Agency retainer (+$6K): Adds high-authority backlink acquisition ($500–$1,000 per niche edit) and specialist debt-finance writers ($450–$1,500 per article). These are the SEO investments that compound — they make Year-2 paid media cheaper.
  • Lead purchases (+$5K): Adds live-call transfers ($60–$75 each, pre-qualified). These are essentially "warm leads delivered to a phone" — they only make sense once the rep team is large enough to take the calls in real time.
  • Tech & SaaS (+$2.5K): More Forth seats as the rep team grows, more soft-credit API queries because more applicants exist, premium hosting because the calculator is taking real traffic.
  • Security & audits (+$2K): Annual pen-test and full compliance audit. These are one-a-year deliverables averaged across the budget — same insurance, more covered surface.
✦

The gate is the price-control mechanism

Stage 3 only triggers after the plan proves it can acquire a customer for less than $100. If a customer is being acquired for less than $100 and worth $5,000 in revenue, scaling the spend isn't risky — it's the obvious move. Worst case, Stage 3 never happens and the 12-month total is $190K, not $319K. The customer is opting into the higher number conditional on the lower one already paying off.

06 · The Comparable · What The Alternatives Cost

Cheap is the most expensive option in this category.

Whenever a customer says "too expensive," the answer is always "compared to what?" Three realistic alternatives for a debt-settlement startup, with the actual numbers.

$335K+

"Just copy NDR's playbook"

National Debt Relief's blended CAC is $335+. Acquiring 1,000 enrollments their way costs $335,000 in media alone — and that's after you've built the brand, the infrastructure, and the team to spend it well. Bulldog can't bid those rates and shouldn't try.

$400–600K

"Just buy real-time leads"

Real-time form leads run $10–$60 each, convert to enrollment at 8–12%. To get 1,000 enrollments you need 8,000–12,000 leads × $50 avg = $400K–$600K in leads alone, with no infrastructure, no retargeting, no compounding moat at the end.

$0

"Just keep the current site"

Stay with the existing brochure site, spend nothing on media, hope for referrals. Real cost: zero new enrollments, no infrastructure built, competitors compound for 12 more months. Marketing CPA inflation is 13.5%/year — every month delayed is a more expensive Year 2.

$319K

Bulldog 360 plan

12-month investment that builds a sub-$100 CPA engine, produces real enrollments inside Q2, sets up white-label productization in Year 2. The expensive option only if you ignore the others.

✦

The reframe to use with the customer

Lead the conversation with "What does it cost you to acquire a customer today, and how do you want that number to look in twelve months?" Not "Here's our $319K proposal." Once the customer is anchored on CAC instead of total spend, the plan reads as the cheap option — because it is.

07 · The Hidden Cost Of Doing Nothing

Standing still is not a free option.

The unspoken comparison in any "this is too expensive" conversation is the do-nothing case — keep the current site, keep current spend, keep current revenue. Three reasons that comparison is wrong.

A

CPA inflation is structural

+13.5% per year, compounding

Paid-search CPA in this category has compounded ~13.5% annually since 2020 and the inflation is structural — more competitors, more bid pressure, no relief in sight. A campaign that costs $100/enrollment today costs $113 next year and $128 the year after. The longer the customer waits, the more expensive Year 1 becomes.

B

The market is growing without them

$6.1B → $11.2B by 2034

The debt-settlement market is on a 6.2% CAGR trajectory. Every month Bulldog isn't capturing enrollments, the demand is finding its way to NDR, Freedom, and Accredited instead. Market share lost during the wait period is share that competitors compound on top of for years.

C

The empty seat fills

The "hybrid" gap won't sit open forever

The entire strategic premise of the plan — the empty seat between B2B lead vendors and B2C providers — is empty today. Some smarter capitalized player notices the same gap in 18 months, builds the same hybrid stack, and Bulldog 360's window closes. The cost of waiting isn't $0; it's "the entire moat."

★

The asymmetric bet

If the plan works: Bulldog 360 captures the empty seat, runs a sub-$100 CPA engine, builds a white-label SaaS pillar in Year 2, and the $319K is the cheapest customer acquisition the company will ever buy. If the plan underperforms but doesn't fail: Stage 3 never triggers, total spend stops at ~$190K, Bulldog still has a working calculator and CRM stack. If the plan fails outright: Bulldog has spent ~$190K to learn that this market is harder than it looks — same lesson NDR paid hundreds of millions to learn. The downside is bounded. The upside is the business.

The four sentences to remember

What to say when the customer says it's too expensive.

If Gary or Tyler is on a call and the customer pushes back on the price, this is the script. Not memorized — internalized.

  1. "Each enrolled customer is worth about $5,000 in revenue over the program's life."This anchors the conversation on per-customer value before per-month cost.
  2. "Your direct competitors pay $335+ to acquire that same customer. This plan is designed to get you below $100."The plan's job is not to spend $319K — it's to make every customer cheaper than your competitors'.
  3. "You only owe 64 enrollments across the entire year to fully repay the 12-month investment."That's about five enrollments per month. Bulldog's pipeline plan calls for three to five per week.
  4. "And the $43K/month Stage 3 number doesn't trigger unless your CPA is already under $100 — meaning you'd be opting into spending more only after the plan has already paid for itself."The biggest scary number on the page is conditional. The customer is in control of whether it ever activates.
